Paper Video
TL;DR: An offline grasp-affordance heatmap selects three-point grasp candidates, a model-based controller reaches them, and a proprioceptive reinforcement-learning policy refines contact and stabilizes the lift. The system reaches 91.9% nominal grasp-and-lift success on eight evaluated objects, compared with 56.1% for naive squeezing.

Project overview

Dexterous grasping couples target selection, reaching, and contact stabilization. This work separates those responsibilities through a compact three-point interface. Geometry-based grasp-affordance heatmaps are precomputed from object meshes, optionally steered toward a language-specified region, and used to select contacts online according to the current hand pose. A model-based reactive controller then tracks the object and guides the hand toward the selected contacts. During the final centimeters, a reinforcement-learning policy uses only finger joint states and recent actions to refine contact, stabilize the grasp, and lift the object.

Results

Steerable affordances

The mustard-bottle pipeline shows heatmap-based target selection and three-point grasp planning from object geometry.

Three-stage execution

The Pringles hardware demo shows reactive reaching, final-centimeter contact adjustment, and grasp stabilization on an unseen object.

Grasp-and-lift evaluation

The Rubik's-cube pipeline provides an additional view of the heatmap and grasp-execution stages behind the reported success results.
